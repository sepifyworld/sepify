How to calculate hours on a time card

For each shift, subtract the start time from the end time, then take off any unpaid break. Add up the days for the week’s total.

  • Example: 8:45 AM to 5:15 PM is 8 hours 30 minutes. Take off a 30-minute unpaid break and the shift is 8 hours.
  • Overnight shifts: 10:00 PM to 6:00 AM is 8 hours. Count from the start to midnight, then from midnight to the end.
  • Overtime: hours above your weekly overtime threshold are paid at the overtime rate. In Ontario, for most jobs, that’s hours over 44 in a week at 1.5 times the regular rate. Other provinces set their own rules, some daily as well as weekly.

Why exact times matter

Rounding each punch to the nearest quarter hour, or writing “9 to 5” when the real times were 9:07 and 4:52, adds up across a team and a year. Missing breaks are the same problem in the other direction. A time clock that records the exact minute, and takes breaks out automatically, removes the guesswork from both sides.

Time card questions.

How do I calculate hours worked with a lunch break?
Subtract the start time from the end time, then subtract the unpaid break. For example, 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM is 8 hours 30 minutes; with a 30-minute unpaid lunch, that’s 8 hours worked.
How do I convert minutes to decimal hours?
Divide the minutes by 60. 15 minutes is 0.25 hours, 30 minutes is 0.5 hours and 45 minutes is 0.75 hours. So 7 hours 45 minutes is 7.75 hours.
How is overtime calculated in Ontario?
For most jobs in Ontario, hours worked over 44 in a work week are overtime, paid at 1.5 times the regular rate. Some jobs have different rules, and other provinces use different thresholds, so check the employment standards that apply to you.
